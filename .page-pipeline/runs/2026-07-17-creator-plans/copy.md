## SECTION 2 — POSITIONING
H2: Most brands don't have a creator plan. They have a creator list.

A spreadsheet. Some macro names the agency knew. A few micros the intern found. One big-ticket signing that ate half the budget. The brief goes out. Content comes back. Some of it looks like the brand paid for it. Some of it flies. Nobody can tell why. Nobody can repeat it.

The problem isn't the creators. It's that no one is planning them like a campaign.

## SECTION 3 — CREATOR CHEMISTRY (bold centerpiece #1, leading the page)
H2 (hero-scale): Creator Chemistry.
Sub: The right brand. The right creator. An idea both sides actually want to make.

Most creator work goes wrong before the money is spent. Someone picks the creator on reach. Someone writes a brief that treats the creator as a delivery vehicle. The creator posts it. Their audience clocks the pitch in three seconds. The brand blames the creator.

Chemistry is what fixes this at the top of the decision, not the bottom of it.

Juice checks three things at once.

**The creator's audience.** Category affinity, spend signals, region, language, the topics their audience already trusts them on. A dermatologist creator whose audience actually asks about actives is worth more to a skincare launch than a lifestyle creator with four times the following.

**The creator's content DNA.** Tone, pace, hook style, the formats their audience watches to completion. A UGC-style creator can carry a demo. A meme creator can carry a category truth. Very few can do both, and most briefs pretend they can.

**The idea.** Written to their content, not to a brand template. Every match comes with the idea Juice would pitch that creator specifically, in a hook their audience recognizes as theirs.

When the three line up, the content converts, the brand safety team stops asking for six rounds of edits, and the creator posts it because they wanted to.

## SECTION 4 — COMPLETE CREATOR PLANS (bold centerpiece #2)
H2 (hero-scale): Complete Creator Plans.
Sub: Reach. Type. Region. Format. Stage. Orchestrated.

A single creator can move a niche. A campaign needs a cast.

Juice plans creators the way a director casts a film. Nano creators go where credibility matters most. Regional creators go where language and cultural context beat reach. Macros go where the story needs to travel. Category insiders go where trust is the hardest thing to buy. Superfans go where retention lives.

Every creator on the board carries a role.

- Reach role — Nano, Micro, Mid, Macro, Category expert
- Regional role — Which language, which market, which cultural moment
- Content role — Hook, demo, review, meme, long-form
- Stage role — Where they land in the campaign arc
- Metric role — What you're measuring them against. Awareness lift. Save rate. Comment quality. Conversion. Share.

The board tells you when a plan is too top-heavy on macros. When a language is missing. When your closing stage has no creators in it. When you're paying for reach in a market where credibility would compound harder for less.

This is what turns creator marketing from an assembly of posts into a campaign.

## SECTION 7 — WHAT JUICE ACTUALLY KNOWS ABOUT CREATORS
H2: Follower count is a starting filter, not a decision.

Most tools stop at reach. Juice looks at the signals that decide whether a creator can actually carry a campaign.

- **Category affinity of the audience.** Whether the followers are people who buy your category, or just people who like the topic. These are not the same thing.
- **Comment-to-view quality.** Ten thoughtful questions in the comments outperform a thousand fire emojis. Juice reads the difference.
- **Save and forward rates.** The two metrics that predict whether a piece of creator content has a second life.
- **Sponsored versus organic performance.** A creator's organic post is not their sponsored post. Juice looks at what happens when they're paid, not when they're free.
- **Regional lift.** A Tamil creator with 40k followers can outperform a national one with two million on the right idea. The dataset shows you where.
- **Content-style fingerprint.** Hook shape, pacing, editing tempo, format mix. The reason briefs get rewritten five times is usually that the first one ignored this.
- **Historical brand collab list.** Which brands actually shipped with them, which ones didn't, and what happened.
- **Cultural moment fit.** Whether they're an early or late mover on a trend.

The output is a creator match that stands up when someone in finance asks you why.
